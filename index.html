How a pick is scored

  • Usage. The player's share of his team's targets, carries and red-zone chances over his last ten games, times how much the team is expected to throw and run. Recent games count more and last season counts half.
  • Game script. The spread and total set the team's expected points. Favorites run more and throw less; higher expected points lift yards and touchdowns. The sizes come from 2,717 team games from 2021 to 2025.
  • Conditions. Outdoor wind above 10 mph lowers passing and rushing efficiency and touchdowns. Home teams score slightly more touchdowns. The opponent's defense this season nudges the result.
  • Injuries and roles. A player listed Out or Doubtful, or missing from his team's depth chart, gets no projection, and his recent share is spread over his teammates. When a starting quarterback is out, his team's receptions drop about 4%, receiving yards about 7% and touchdowns about 10%, the average from games started by backups since 2021 after allowing for the spread. A quarterback whose snap share has risen is projected at his new role.
  • Defensive starters out. A defensive starter is a player who took at least 60% of his team's defensive snaps in two of its last four games. Fitted on 4,408 team games from 2016 to 2025 (snap counts and team totals, against what each offense and defense averaged), each front-seven starter out (linemen and linebackers) added about 4% to the opponent's rushing yards and about 3% to its touchdowns. Missing cornerbacks and safeties made no measurable difference to passing yards or catches (under 1%, within the noise). The board uses those sizes pulled toward zero by their uncertainty: per front-seven starter out, rushing yards +3.6% and anytime touchdowns +2.3%; passing props move by well under 1%. At most three per unit count. A starter is out when the report or Claude's call says so, or when he missed his last game and is on no report this week. Defensive starters are not listed under Game conditions and injuries; the Update injuries run makes the calls on them. Checked on the 2025 season while it was held out of the fit: touchdowns did rise in games with front-seven starters out (about 7%), but rushing yards did not, and the adjustment left overall forecast error unchanged. Treat it as a small nudge, not an edge on its own.
  • Injury watch. A prop is highlighted when the player or a teammate who matters to it has an open status: questionable, doubtful, or missing or limited in practice, and not settled by your own tick. For a teammate, the board re-runs the projection as if he sits (or plays, if he is assumed out) and shows the new projection and model chance when the chance moves by 3 points or more. For the player himself it says so; sportsbooks void a prop if he does not play at all, but a short day still loses an over. Official Out players and players off the depth chart count as settled. Tick Hide injury watch to leave these props off the board.
  • Players coming back. A player returning after missing games (from injury, a suspension, or a reserve or exempt list) is on no injury report, so mark him back this week under Injuries, or let the injury update mark him from the news. He is projected from his games before he left, at about 90% of his old share of targets and carries after missing two or three games and 85% after a longer absence or across an offseason. Teammates give back what they gained while he was out: each one's share is cut by the returner's old share times how much of his recent sample was played without him, 0.8 per point of carries among backs and 1.1 per point of targets. Both sizes come from 514 returns by regular starters in 2016 to 2025; checked on 2024 and 2025 while held out, the adjustment cut the error on backup running backs' carry share by about a fifth.
  • Backups who start. When a regular starter is out and a player who has not been a regular starter (fewer than three starts in his last six games) moves into the starting job (he is listed below the starting spots on the depth chart, and moves up to the top quarterback, running back or tight end, or a top-three receiver once the injured players are taken out), he is projected only from games he started, from this season and last, on any team. A start means a full game at the top of his position: most carries for a running back, most throws and at least 70% of the snaps for a quarterback, top snap share for a tight end, or top three in snaps for a receiver, with at least about half the snaps so relief duty after an injury does not count. His games as a backup are left out, and the injured starter's share is not added on top, because his starts already show what he does with the job. With fewer than two starts on record, the board falls back to his backup games scaled up for the injured starter's share, and says so under the projection.
  • Model chance. The projection becomes a chance of clearing the line, using the usual spread of outcomes for that stat.
  • Market chance. The book's two prices with its margin removed. This is usually the better estimate.
  • Game lines. Each team has an offense and a defense rating in points, updated after every game since 2002 and pulled a third of the way back to average each season. The projection adds a 2-point home edge and takes about 4.5 points off a team whose starting quarterback is out. The model's chance is blended with the market's by the Trust in model setting on the Game lines tab. Totals use the market only.
  • Edge. Model and market are blended by the Trust in model setting. Edge is that blended chance minus the chance implied by the sportsbook's own price for that prop, with the book's margin left in (a -120 price needs 54.5%). The figure under it is the expected return per $1 at that price. Game lines work the same way.

Anytime touchdown has only a Yes price, so the book's margin is estimated: about 5% on favorites, rising to about 50% on longshots.

What to keep in mind

  • Game logs and depth charts are a snapshot from the stats date. Update injuries, under Game conditions and injuries on the board, reads the official NFL.com report for the teams with games left this week, then makes a best guess from current news on which doubtful and questionable players will sit. It is still a guess: it does not see game-day inactives announced 90 minutes before kickoff, so check late scratches yourself.
  • Some picks get no model number on purpose: players who are out, backup quarterbacks (unless he is starting and has at least two starts on record), players with no games this season or on a new team (unless marked back this week), and touchdown longshots at +600 or longer. The game log says too little about them.
  • The biggest edges are where the model disagrees most with the market, and that is also where the model is most likely to be wrong. Trust in model starts at 30% for that reason.
  • Lines are whatever Parlay API (or The Odds API) returned at the moment you pulled. Check a price against your sportsbook before you bet it.
  • Game-line model, tested on 1,693 games from 2020 to 2025 that it was not fitted on: it missed the final margin by 10.1 points on average against the closing spread's 9.8, and its totals did worse than a coin flip. Where it disagreed with the spread by 4 or more points it went 177-143, which is too few games to trust and partly depends on knowing in hindsight who each team's main quarterback was. Treat its edge as a second opinion.
  • The Backtest tab grades picks saved before kickoff against final box scores. A pick is saved only if you open this page after pulling a game's lines and before it kicks off. Props for players who did not play are left out, as a sportsbook would void them.
  • Wind is not forecast for you. Enter it for outdoor games; indoor games ignore it.
  • Rest days and cold were tested and showed no reliable effect, so they are shown but carry no weight.
  • Tested on the 2025 season, weeks 5 to 18: this version's projections missed by less than the first version's on five of six prop types and matched it on passing yards. The gains are a few percent, largest on anytime touchdown.

Nothing here guarantees a win.
